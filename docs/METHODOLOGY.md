# Functional analysis and the House of Quality

Qualitree renders relationships; it does not claim that one vocabulary fits every design method.

| Concept | Classic House of Quality | Functional example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User outcome | Customer attribute: “easy to close” | Need: “enjoy good coffee at home” |
| Engineering response | Measurable characteristic: closing energy | Function: “heat water,” with temperature and warm-up measures |
| Realization | Part characteristics and subsequent deployment | Component: heater assembly; technology: thermoblock or boiler |
| Conflicts | Interactions among engineering characteristics | Interactions among stated functional performance targets |
| Deployment | Characteristics → parts → process/production planning | Needs → functions → components, with further stages optional |

Hauser and Clausing describe measurable engineering characteristics and customer perceptions as distinct parts of the house. They also describe adapting its contents to the team. Consequently, changing only an axis title is not enough to make a function measurable: add a criterion, unit, target, and direction. [The House of Quality, 1988](https://blogs.ubc.ca/nvdteamb/files/2013/10/7-The-House-of-Quality.pdf)

The functional basis literature supports naming functions consistently through their operations and flows. “Heat water” states an action; “heater” names a realization. “Stepper motor” is a technology candidate, not a customer need. [NIST functional basis](https://www.nist.gov/publications/functional-basis-engineering-design-reconciling-and-evolving-previous-efforts)

## Applying the distinction to home coffee

The alternative being displaced is the local café service. At home, the user takes on preparation, storage, cleaning, maintenance and household risks. A sealed-dose system may preserve flavor before the machine is used; that function belongs partly to packaging and supply, not just the machine.

Taste, acceptable drink temperature and household safety are acceptance gates. A favorable weighted priority score cannot compensate for missing one. Brew-water temperature and the temperature of the served drink are different measures.

Weights, relationship strengths and roof signs in the worked example are illustrative workshop judgments. They are not customer survey findings or verified technical correlations. Numerical targets marked with an asterisk are hypotheses for validation. The example includes no fabricated measured competitive scores.

## Reading the roof

The roof records whether improving one specified performance objective helps or hinders another. State which direction counts as improvement. A raw physical correlation is not automatically a favorable design relationship: if one quantity should increase and another should decrease, the interpretation can reverse.

Symbols encode four qualitative strengths, not statistical correlation coefficients. Circle-plus and circle-minus mean strong positive and strong negative. Plain +/− mean positive and negative. Relationships in the body use 9/3/1 separately; those meanings must not be mixed with roof signs or revision markers.

## Revision versus substitution

A revision diff compares two records of the same evolving design. Stable IDs carry identity through reordering and renaming. Green/red/yellow describe additions/removals/modifications, not quality or success.

A substitution analysis compares ways to fulfill needs, such as café service versus home preparation. That calls for explicit acceptance criteria and evidence. It is not inferred from green or red cells in a revision view.
